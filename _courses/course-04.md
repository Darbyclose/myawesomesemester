## About this course

Appalachian Communities looks at the history, culture, and people of Appalachia and what it really means to be part of an Appalachian community. We talk about identity, stereotypes, social class, art, and the different issues that have shaped the region. I am taking this class for my Sociology minor, but I also thought it would be interesting since I have lived in Southwest Virginia for the past few years. I am hoping to learn more about the communities and history of the area around Virginia Tech.

## About Sarah Plummer

Sarah Plummer is a Mellon Postdoctoral Associate in Virginia Tech's Department of Religion and Culture. She earned her Ph.D. from Virginia Tech and has a background in journalism and Appalachian studies. Her work focuses a lot on the ways people use art, performance, and public spaces to tell stories and bring attention to histories that may have been overlooked.

### Research interests

Professor Plummer's research interests include performance, objects, puppetry, protest art, and art in public places. Much of her work examines how art and performance can be used to protest, build community, and tell stories. Her research also connects to Appalachia and the ways people in the region represent their history and identity.

### Publications

- "Creating Puppet Solidarities"
- "Puppetry and Protest"
- Research on Bread and Puppet Theater and the use of puppetry and performing objects in protest

### Service and outreach

Professor Plummer has been involved with Monuments Across Appalachian Virginia, a Virginia Tech project that works with communities to tell Appalachian stories often left out of traditional histories. She has also worked with Virginia Tech students on community research, including a project on African American Union soldiers and how their history could be better represented in public spaces.
